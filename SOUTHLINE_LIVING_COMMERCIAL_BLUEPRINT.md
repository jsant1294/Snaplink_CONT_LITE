# Southline Living Commercial Blueprint

## Current product truth

Southline Living can truthfully be sold as a local-services and professional-visibility platform with a real public marketplace experience, a contractor lead-capture workflow, an operator/content-management layer, and a real-estate operations platform. The strongest present proof is the homeowner-to-contractor booking flow, the contractor dashboard, and the admin/CMS experience documented in the completed audit.

What should be stated plainly:
- homeowners can discover local professionals and submit project requests,
- contractors can receive and manage leads in a workspace,
- operators can publish and manage public content,
- and the platform has a credible real-estate operations layer.

What should not be overstated:
- full marketplace liquidity across every category,
- fully automated invoicing and money workflows for all users,
- seamless universal cross-module integration,
- or enterprise-grade rollout across all verticals.

## Best first offer

The best first offer is a contractor lead-generation and local presence package for home-service professionals.

Recommended structure:
- Setup fee: a one-time onboarding and launch fee for profile setup, service mapping, and initial publishing.
- Monthly subscription: a recurring professional plan for profile visibility, lead intake, and workspace access.
- Optional add-ons: campaign or Flipbook modules and later premium module unlocks.

Because no exact pricing was evidenced in the audit, the pricing guidance should be strategic:
- Setup fee: suggest a range of roughly $750 to $2,000 for a first-wave launch package.
- Monthly subscription: suggest a range of roughly $149 to $399 per month.
- Introductory offer: offer the first month at a discount or reduce the setup fee for the first customers.
- Renewal value: position the renewal around continued lead intake, profile visibility, and ongoing access to the professional workspace.

## Ideal first customer

The ideal first customer is a local home-service contractor who needs more inbound opportunities and a more polished online presence.

Best initial segments:
- remodelers,
- painters,
- landscapers,
- plumbers,
- electricians,
- and handymen.

These customers have the clearest fit because the audit shows a relevant service catalog, a booking flow, and a contractor dashboard that directly support lead capture and local visibility.

## Customer problem

The customer problem is simple and urgent: local professionals often depend on referrals, word of mouth, or fragmented online presence, while homeowners are actively searching for trusted local help. The platform solves the gap between discovery and action by giving contractors a visible profile and a structured lead intake path.

## Core promise

Southline Living helps local professionals get found by nearby homeowners, receive new project inquiries, and manage the first customer conversation from one simple workspace.

## Included modules

The first offer should include:
- public professional profile publishing,
- local discovery presence,
- booking and lead capture,
- contractor dashboard access,
- basic profile setup and onboarding,
- and one clear lead-management workflow.

These are the modules with the strongest evidence and the clearest commercial fit.

## Operator setup workflow

The operator must perform a simple, repeatable setup workflow:
1. confirm the contractor category and service list,
2. publish the contractor profile and local discovery entry,
3. enable the booking and lead intake flow,
4. verify the contractor dashboard access and lead board,
5. activate any first add-on such as a campaign or Flipbook,
6. review the initial homeowner experience before launch.

This workflow should be packaged as a setup service rather than a vague self-serve experience.

## Contractor workflow

Once the setup is complete, the contractor should be able to:
- review a new lead,
- see project details submitted by the homeowner,
- update the lead status,
- respond to the inquiry,
- manage the next step from the dashboard,
- and optionally upgrade to a marketing add-on.

The product should feel practical and operational, not purely promotional.

## Homeowner workflow

The homeowner should be able to:
- discover a professional through the public marketplace or homepage,
- review the profile and service information,
- request a consultation or project inquiry,
- submit project details through the booking flow,
- and create a lead for the contractor.

This is the clearest and most defensible end-to-end workflow to sell today.

## Pricing model

### Setup fee
- Recommended as a one-time launch fee covering onboarding, profile setup, service mapping, and initial activation.
- Strategic range: roughly $750 to $2,000.

### Monthly subscription
- Recommended as the core recurring revenue model for profile visibility, workspace access, and lead management.
- Strategic range: roughly $149 to $399 per month.

### Optional add-ons
- campaign add-on,
- Flipbook add-on,
- featured visibility or profile prominence,
- and later premium modules like money or invoicing once they are more mature.

### Introductory offer
- First-month discount, reduced setup fee, or a limited-time starter package for the first customers.

### Renewal value
- Renewal should emphasize continuity of visibility, lead intake, and ongoing access to the professional workspace rather than vague “platform access.”

## Immediate upsells

The immediate upsells should be:
- a campaign add-on,
- a Flipbook add-on,
- a promoted profile or featured visibility option,
- and a premium module unlock once the contractor is successfully receiving leads.

These upsells are natural because they extend the same core use case: visibility plus lead flow.

## SnapLink cross-sells

SnapLink should be positioned as the shared platform layer behind the offer.

The strongest cross-sells are:
- a branded contractor profile experience,
- a campaign or Flipbook experience tied to the profile,
- and a professional package that combines local visibility with lead capture and a polished customer-facing presence.

## Demonstration script

A three-minute demonstration should follow this sequence:

1. 0:00-0:40 — Show the public homepage and the local discovery experience.
2. 0:40-1:20 — Open the homeowner booking path and show how a project inquiry is submitted.
3. 1:20-2:00 — Switch to the contractor dashboard and show the lead appearing in the workspace.
4. 2:00-2:30 — Show the contractor profile setup and the ability to manage the lead and update status.
5. 2:30-3:00 — Explain the monthly plan, the setup fee, and the optional campaign or Flipbook add-on.

The demo should feel grounded in real workflow rather than abstract product features.

## Claims safe now

These claims are safe to make:
- Southline Living offers a public marketplace and contractor discovery experience.
- Contractors can receive and manage leads through a workspace.
- The platform includes a CMS and operator console for public content management.
- The platform has a credible real-estate operations layer.
- SnapLink is positioned as the platform layer behind contractor profiles and lead-generation workflows.

## Claims requiring qualification

These should be stated carefully:
- fully automated invoicing and money workflows,
- complete marketplace liquidity for every service category,
- seamless cross-module integration across all product surfaces,
- and enterprise-grade white-label or multi-tenant rollout.

## Claims to avoid

These should be avoided:
- “We are a fully mature marketplace with unlimited demand.”
- “All leads are instantly converted.”
- “Every contractor will see immediate ROI.”
- “The platform is fully automated across every module.”

## Launch blockers

The main launch blockers are:
- the need for a clearly packaged first offer with a defined onboarding workflow,
- the need for at least one polished contractor profile and one realistic lead example,
- the need to keep the first launch focused on one strong use case instead of broad platform complexity,
- and the need to avoid presenting unfinished or gated modules as though they are universally ready.
